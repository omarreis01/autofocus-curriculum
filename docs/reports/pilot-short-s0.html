Answer to the research question

Does the network choose blurry/low-contrast first? No. No teacher we tried does this. Raw learning progress (PG, SPG) drives the teacher to the hardest arm. Relative progress (PG ÷ loss) and ALP-GMM stay near uniform.

Does it give robustness? Exposure to blur gives robustness: CIFAR-10-C, adversarial PGD, and a stronger low-frequency (shape-like) bias. The order of the exposure does not add anything: random-per-batch is as good as or better than every teacher and every hand schedule.

Findings

  1. Hard-first comes from the reward scale. With raw PG the teacher puts 71–74% of batches on the hardest arm in the middle of training. With relative PG (teacher_rpg) it puts 1–3% there and stays near uniform. η changes only the speed (η=0.003 slower, η=0.03 faster and swings back). Without importance weighting the drift is slow (13–14% late).
  2. Near-uniform teachers are as good as random. teacher_rpg 90.0 / 81.1 / 82.4 and ALP-GMM 89.0 / 82.1 / 82.0 vs random 89.4 / 81.4 / 82.2 (clean / C-seen / C-unseen, 30 ep). Hard-first teachers lose 3–5 points clean.
  3. Blur is the axis that matters. random_blur (contrast fixed at 1) gets 82.1 / 81.9 C-seen/unseen. random_contrast (no blur) gets 68.2 / 73.6, the same as sharp.
  4. Contrast becomes real only per image or without BatchNorm. CIFAR-10-C contrast: random per batch 69.3, random per image 88.9, sharp with GroupNorm 87.6, sharp with BN 73.8. With BN, a batch-wide contrast change is normalized away during training.
  5. Shape bias follows exposure, not order. Share of hybrid images classified by their low-frequency part (σ=2): sharp 6%, random 23%, teacher_pg 54%, shuffled (same arms, random order) 60%. At 100 epochs: teacher_pg 67%, shuffled 69%. The longer the model sees heavy blur, the higher the bias.
  6. Blur training helps small adversarial attacks. PGD-10 at ε=1/255: sharp 34.8%, random 59.5%, teacher_pg_noiw 59.7%, teacher_pg 49.3%. Reverse (blur at the end) is worst: 26.7%. random_image is low (37.0%) although its CIFAR-10-C is high.
  7. There is a trade-off. Models with a high shape bias lose high-frequency accuracy: high-pass σ=1 accuracy sharp 80%, teacher_pg 40%, shuffled 36%.
  8. Hand schedule length: a longer blurry phase (staircase 75%) gives more robustness (78.9 / 80.3) and less clean accuracy (88.5); 25% is close to sharp.

Caveats

FGSM looks too weak. For sharp 100 ep, FGSM at 2/255 (53.6%) is higher than PGD at 1/255 (44.9%). Likely gradient masking on very confident models. Use PGD numbers only.
Shape bias is a CIFAR proxy. Low-frequency of image A + high-frequency of image B, not Geirhos style transfer. It measures low- vs high-frequency reliance.
ALP-GMM is quantized to the nearest of the 16 arms; its continuous samples are logged.
GroupNorm costs clean accuracy at 30 epochs (sharp 90.2 vs 93.2 with BN). GN results compare within GN only.
random_image arm log stores only the first image's arm per batch (unbiased histogram).
