Summary

  • BatchNorm mismatch. Each batch uses one arm. In training, BN normalizes each batch with its own statistics. At test time, BN uses running averages that mix all arms. Clean accuracy falls to 13–60% for mixed conditions. After a BN recalibration on clean training images (100 batches, no test data), the numbers become normal.
  • The teacher does not choose blurry-first. It starts near uniform and moves to the most degraded arm (σ=3, c=0.2). This is the opposite of H1. One seed only.
  • Robustness comes from exposure. After recalibration, random, teacher and shuffled have similar CIFAR-10-C scores (~79–82%). Order adds no clear gain in this pilot. Sharp gets 69/73.

Decision: how to fix the BatchNorm mismatch

A. Recalibrate BN on clean training images before every eval (recommended). Same rule for all 7 conditions. Uses training data only. No retraining: the pilot numbers above already use it. Small change in src/eval.py. Undo: a flag.
B. Replace BN with GroupNorm. Train and test behave the same. All runs must be retrained, and the sharp baseline changes. GroupNorm also cancels most of the contrast change per image.
C. Mix arms inside a batch. The teacher picks a distribution, not one arm. Changes the method and the Graves reward.

One more effect stays with A: during training, BN in train mode undoes most of a global contrast change. So the contrast axis is weaker than the blur axis. The teacher's choices along the diagonal are mostly driven by blur.

Next step after the decision

  1. Add BN recalibration to src/eval.py (option A) and re-run the eval of the pilot.
  2. F5: 7 conditions × 3 seeds × 30 epochs (~35 min on the A100).
  3. Check if the "move to hard arms" pattern repeats over seeds.
