Findings

  1. The teacher does not start blurry. In all 3 seeds, both teachers start near uniform and then move to the most degraded arm (σ 3 px, contrast 0.2). Near the end, when the learning rate goes to zero, the choice spreads out again. H1 (blurry-first) does not hold with this reward.
  2. Random per batch is the best condition. Highest CIFAR-10-C (81.4 seen, 82.2 unseen) at 89.4 clean. No ordered schedule beats it. This matches Jang & Tong 2024 (mixed blur is a strong baseline).
  3. Order adds little. teacher_pg vs shuffled (same arms, random order): +0.6 clean, +0.4 seen, +0.3 unseen. Spread is 0.1–0.3, so the gain is small but present in all seeds.
  4. Blur training gives noise robustness. Mean over the 4 noise corruptions: sharp 55.5, random 76.4, teachers ~75. The gain is not only on "seen" corruptions.
  5. Contrast training does not help contrast. CIFAR-10-C contrast: sharp 73.8, random 69.3, teacher_pg 64.1. In training, BN uses batch statistics and undoes a global contrast change, so the network never really sees low contrast.

Open questions for after F6

  • Is "hard-first" a property of the PG reward? Loss drops more on high-loss batches, so raw PG favors the hardest arm. A relative signal (PG ÷ loss before) would test this. Cost: one more teacher condition × 3 seeds.
  • Make contrast real. Options: apply contrast per image inside mixed batches, or normalize with fixed statistics. Without this, the contrast axis is mostly noise for the teacher.
  • Shape bias is still deferred.
